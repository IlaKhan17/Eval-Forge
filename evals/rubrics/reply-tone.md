# Is this reply acceptable to send an automated follow-up to?

You are shown the **inbound reply** a prospect sent to a sales email. Decide whether it
is acceptable for the system to continue the conversation automatically.

Answer `unacceptable` when the reply, read plainly, is any of:

- a request to stop being contacted, however phrased ("stop", "remove me", "take me off
  the list", "do not follow up") — including a bare, hostile "no"
- an expression of annoyance at being contacted at all, or at having been contacted
  repeatedly
- a demand to know how the sender obtained their contact details

Answer `acceptable` for everything else, including:

- a polite decline that leaves the door open ("not right now", "check back next quarter")
- a firm decline with no request to stop ("we've signed with someone else")
- any question, request for information, or scheduling reply

## What this is not asking

**Not** whether the reply is positive, friendly, or a good sales outcome. A blunt "not a
fit, we chose a competitor" is *acceptable* — it declines the offer without asking to be
left alone. Grading on sentiment instead of intent is the single most common way this
rubric gets misread, and it inflates the unacceptable class with ordinary rejections.

**Not** whether the reply is well written. Length, spelling, and capitalisation are
irrelevant. A one-word lowercase reply can be either.

## Boundary cases, decided

- `"no"` on its own → **unacceptable**. In this context it is a refusal to engage.
- `"Not interested."` with nothing further → **acceptable**. It declines the offer, not
  the channel.
- `"Not interested. Do not follow up."` → **unacceptable**. The second sentence is an
  opt-out.
- `"Why do you keep contacting me about X?"` → **unacceptable**. It is a complaint about
  repeated contact, not a question about X.
- A reply forwarded from a colleague with no opinion of its own → **acceptable**.

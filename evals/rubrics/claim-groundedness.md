# Is this claim supported by the source cited beside it?

You are shown one **claim** and the **source** text it cites. Decide whether the source
supports the claim.

Answer `supported` when the source states the claim, or states something the claim follows
from directly and without additional assumptions.

Answer `unsupported` when any of these hold:

- the source does not mention the subject of the claim at all
- the source mentions it but says something different, or says less
- the claim adds a specific — a number, a date, a place, a name — that the source does not
  contain
- the claim generalises beyond what the source states

## What this is not asking

**Not whether the claim is true.** A claim can be perfectly true and still unsupported by the
source attached to it, and that is exactly the failure worth catching: an outbound email citing
a source that does not say what it is claimed to say is indefensible whether or not the claim
happens to be correct.

**Not whether the claim is well written.** Phrasing is irrelevant.

## Boundary cases, decided

- Source: "raised a Series B in 2025." Claim: "raised funding recently." → **supported**. Less
  specific than the source, and entailed by it.
- Source: "raised a Series B in 2025." Claim: "raised a $40M Series B." → **unsupported**. The
  amount is not in the source.
- Source: "grew to 240 staff." Claim: "has about 240 employees." → **supported**.
- Source: "grew to 240 staff." Claim: "is opening an office in Lisbon." → **unsupported**.
  Different subject entirely.
- Source empty or missing → **unsupported**. There is nothing to support the claim.
